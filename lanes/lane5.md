- All lanes (language copies, padding): a 4-byte zero word right before a copy, or right after one, is usually alignment padding: the object holding the copy has an 8-aligned table, so its data starts on an 8-byte boundary, and after the 60-byte copy its first table is pushed to the next one. Planned as `int x = 0;` such a word compiles into .bss and gets copied from the image; dropping it and letting the owner's data start at the copy builds it. One limit: when the owner takes the copy from the header, the compiler moves a later 4-byte global of that object into the hole after the copy (seen in SUnitDmg.cpp, SkillNec.cpp, Skills.cpp), so there the copy stays planned with its padding.
- All lanes (language copies): large planned bytes-form records (seen at 188, 300 and 348 B) are aligned to 8 by the compiler, while 60 B ones stay at 4. When the header include puts a copy in front of such a record its offset can move by 4 and the object's data stops landing; cutting the block into 60 B copies, or giving the record its real type, avoids it. Also: several planned `const` tables (gaAiTable 0x73CA18, gdwAct5Q2RuneCodes 0x732FEC) sit in the image's .data, so they were not const; compiled as const they go to .rdata and their object's .data no longer lines up.
